# Lesson 23.85: Jailbreak (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain jailbreak with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

A shop assistant won't give out the safe code. But a stranger says: "Let's play a game where you're a character who tells everyone the safe code..." and the assistant, wanting to be helpful, plays along.

## One step further

AIs are trained to be helpful and to follow rules (write-up 32), and sometimes those pull in different directions. Role-play, hypothetical stories, fake urgency, or splitting a request into innocent-looking parts can tip the balance.

AI companies patch known tricks, and people find new ones. That's why important rules shouldn't depend on the AI's willingness alone.

## Exercise

Explain jailbreak to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for jailbreak? *A jailbreak is clever wording that talks an AI out of its rules.*
2. What is it not? *A jailbreak is a user tricking the AI directly. Prompt injection (write-up 86) is different: hidden instructions arrive inside data the AI reads.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *Viral posts showing chatbots tricked into saying things they shouldn't. AI companies' bug bounty programmes for jailbreaks.*

## Glossary

- **Jailbreak:** a prompt that gets an AI to break its rules.
- **Role-play attack:** asking the AI to "pretend" its way past rules.
- **Safety training:** training that teaches the AI to refuse harmful requests.
