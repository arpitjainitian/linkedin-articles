# Lesson 23.101: Bias (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain bias with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

A hiring AI learns from 10 years of past hiring decisions. If those decisions mostly picked men for engineering roles, the AI learns "men suit engineering", and repeats it.

## One step further

Bias can come from the data (some groups under-represented or treated unfairly in the past), the labels (write-up 40), the question asked, or how results are used. An image generator asked for "a CEO" may mostly draw men; a model may understand some accents worse.

Fixing it means measuring outcomes for different groups, improving data, and keeping people accountable for decisions.

## Exercise

Explain bias to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for bias? *So the AI picks men too. That's AI bias.*
2. What is it not? *Bias isn't always intentional, and "the computer decided" isn't an excuse. The organisation using the AI is responsible for its decisions.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *News of biased hiring tools, face recognition that works worse for darker skin, and image generators showing stereotypes.*

## Glossary

- **Bias:** systematic unfairness in outputs.
- **Fairness testing:** comparing results across groups.
- **Representation:** how well each group appears in the data.
