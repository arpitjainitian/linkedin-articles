# Lesson 23.28: Structured output (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain structured output with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

Ask an AI to read a receipt and you get a friendly paragraph. Your accounting system can't read a paragraph. It needs boxes: date, merchant, amount.

## One step further

You describe the form (called a schema): "date as a date, amount as a number, merchant as text". Many AI services can now guarantee the answer matches that form exactly, so software can use it directly.

The format being right doesn't mean the content is right. ₹1,180 can still be read as ₹118 (Series 2 covers exact data).

## Exercise

Explain structured output to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for structured output? *Structured output makes the AI fill in boxes instead of writing paragraphs.*
2. What is it not? *A perfectly shaped answer can still be wrong inside. Check the values, not just the format.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *Apps that turn a photo of a receipt or ID card into filled-in fields. AI features that create calendar events from emails.*

## Glossary

- **Schema:** the description of the boxes and their types.
- **JSON:** a common text format for structured data.
- **Field:** one named box, like "amount".
