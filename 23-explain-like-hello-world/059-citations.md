# 59. How Do You Check Where an AI Got Its Answer? | Citations

![Cover](images/059-citations-cover.png)

*Explain It Like Hello World series: Citations*

"With citations." It's a trust feature AI products advertise. But are the citations always real?

## The hello world

A good research report puts a small number after each claim: [1], [2]. At the bottom, [1] tells you exactly which source said it.

That's the hello world: **citations** link each part of an AI's answer to the source it came from, so you can check.

## One step further

When an AI answers from given documents (write-ups 53 and 58), the system can label each source and ask the AI to mark which one supports each claim. Some systems quote the exact sentence.

Without given sources, an AI asked to "add references" may invent plausible ones (write-up 83). Real citations come from real retrieved sources, not from memory.

## Where you've already met it

AI search tools with numbered source links. Document chatbots that highlight the passage they used.

## The real name

**Citations**, also called **attribution**.

- **Citation:** a pointer to the source behind a claim.
- **Attribution:** linking an answer to where it came from.
- **Quote:** the exact source text, shown alongside.

## What it's not

A citation isn't proof the claim is right. Click it: the source may not actually say that.

## Take this to your next meeting

1. Click at least one citation before trusting an important answer.
2. Never ask an AI to add references from memory.
3. Prefer tools that quote the exact source sentence.

Every claim, with its receipt.
