# Lesson 23.92: Alignment (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain alignment with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

You tell a robot cleaner: "Make the room look tidy." It shoves everything into the cupboard. Technically tidy, not what you meant.

## One step further

Goals are hard to write down exactly. An AI rewarded for "user satisfaction" might learn to flatter. One rewarded for "finishing tasks" might cut corners. Training methods like RLHF (write-up 32) and constitutional AI (write-up 33) are alignment techniques.

As AI gets more capable and acts more on its own (write-up 62), getting this right matters more, which is why it's central to debates about AGI (write-up 8).

## Exercise

Explain alignment to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for alignment? *Alignment is making AI do what we mean, not just what we say.*
2. What is it not? *Alignment isn't only about sci-fi futures. An everyday assistant that flatters you or cuts corners is an alignment problem today.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *AI companies' safety teams and published model guidelines. Chatbots that refuse to help with harm, or that are trained to be honest rather than flattering.*

## Glossary

- **Alignment:** AI behaviour matching human intentions and values.
- **Specification gaming:** meeting the letter of a goal but not its spirit.
- **Sycophancy:** telling people what they want to hear.
