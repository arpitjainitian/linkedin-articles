# Lesson 23.102: Explainability (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain explainability with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

A bank rejects your loan. "Computer says no" isn't acceptable. "Your income is below the limit for this amount, and you've missed two payments this year" is.

## One step further

Simple models can show which factors mattered most. Large AI models are much harder: billions of knobs (write-up 28), no simple list of reasons. An LLM can write an explanation, but that explanation isn't guaranteed to be the real reason.

So for decisions that matter, teams often let code make the decision with clear rules, and use AI to explain it (Series 3), while researchers work on looking inside models.

## Exercise

Explain explainability to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for explainability? *Explainability is being able to say why, in plain words.*
2. What is it not? *An AI's confident explanation isn't proof of its reasoning. And "it's a black box" isn't an excuse for decisions that affect people.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *Loan, insurance and hiring decisions where you have a right to a reason. "Why am I seeing this?" buttons on ads and feeds. My [Today in AI No. 006](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/today-ai-006-your-made-decision-can-you-prove-why-data-arpit-jain-vjenf) asks how you prove why an AI made a decision.*

## Glossary

- **Explainability:** giving understandable reasons for a decision.
- **Interpretability:** understanding what happens inside a model.
- **Feature importance:** which inputs mattered most.
