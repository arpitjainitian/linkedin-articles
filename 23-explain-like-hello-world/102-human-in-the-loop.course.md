# Lesson 23.102: Human-in-the-loop (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain human-in-the-loop with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

An autopilot flies the plane, but a pilot watches, approves big changes and can take the controls at any time.

## One step further

The AI does the bulk of the work: drafts, sorts, suggests. A person steps in where mistakes are costly: approving a refund, sending a legal letter, a medical decision. Their corrections can also improve the system over time.

The risk is rubber-stamping: if one person approves hundreds of items an hour, they stop really checking. Good designs send people only the cases that need judgement.

## Exercise

Explain human-in-the-loop to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for human-in-the-loop? *Human-in-the-loop means a person checks the AI before it counts.*
2. What is it not? *A human somewhere in the process isn't enough. They need time, information and the power to say no.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *"Approve before sending" buttons in email assistants, and loan or claims decisions reviewed by staff.*

## Glossary

- **Approval step:** a point where a person must say yes.
- **Rubber-stamping:** approving without really checking.
- **Escalation:** passing a case up to a person.
