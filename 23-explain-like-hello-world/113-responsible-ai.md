# 113. What Does "Responsible AI" Actually Involve? | Responsible AI

![Cover](images/113-responsible-ai-cover.png)

*Explain It Like Hello World series: Responsible AI*

"Responsible AI." "AI governance." Every company says it does it. What does it actually involve?

## The hello world

Food safety in a restaurant isn't one rule. It's a system: fresh ingredients, clean kitchens, allergy labels, inspections, and someone in charge when something goes wrong.

That's the hello world: **responsible AI** is the set of practices that keep AI fair, safe, private, explainable and accountable, with named owners. **AI governance** is how a company runs them.

## One step further

In practice: a list of where AI is used, risk checks before launch, tests for bias (write-up 108) and safety (write-up 105), privacy rules, explanations for decisions (write-up 109), human review where needed, and a way to report and fix problems.

Regulations (write-up 112) turn many of these into legal duties. Standards such as ISO/IEC 42001 describe how to run them as a management system.

## Where you've already met it

Company AI principles pages, AI review boards, and risk forms teams fill in before launching an AI feature.

## The real name

**Responsible AI**, run through **AI governance**.

- **AI governance:** the rules, roles and checks for using AI.
- **AI inventory:** a list of every AI system in use.
- **Accountability:** a named person answers for the outcome.

## What it's not

Responsible AI isn't a principles page. Without owners, checks and records, it's just words.

## Take this to your next meeting

1. Keep a list of every AI system you use.
2. Name an owner for each one.
3. Check risks before launch, not after a complaint.

Not a poster on the wall. A system with owners.
