# Lesson 23.105: AI regulation (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain AI regulation with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

Toys, cars and medicines all have safety rules, stricter for riskier products. A toy car needs less paperwork than a real one.

## One step further

The EU AI Act is the best-known example. It sorts AI uses into risk levels: some uses are banned (like social scoring by governments), high-risk uses (like AI in hiring, credit or medical devices) need testing, documentation and human oversight, and some uses need transparency, like telling people they're talking to an AI. Its rules apply in stages over several years.

Other countries take different approaches, from voluntary guidelines to sector rules, and data protection laws (like India's DPDP Act) also apply to AI.

## Exercise

Explain AI regulation to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for AI regulation? *AI regulation works the same way: rules by level of risk.*
2. What is it not? *Regulation isn't only for AI builders. Companies using AI, especially for decisions about people, have duties too. (This is general information, not legal advice.)*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *Notices saying "you are chatting with an AI". Company AI policies and new compliance roles.*

## Glossary

- **Risk-based regulation:** stricter rules for riskier uses.
- **High-risk AI:** uses that can seriously affect people's lives or rights.
- **Transparency obligation:** a duty to tell people AI is involved.
