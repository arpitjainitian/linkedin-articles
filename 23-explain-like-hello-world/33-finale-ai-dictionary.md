# 33. Your AI Dictionary, One Line per Word | The Finale

![Cover](images/33-finale-ai-dictionary-cover.png)

*Explain It Like Hello World series: the finale*

Remember the meeting from the curtain raiser? "We'll use a quantized mixture-of-experts model with a bigger context window."

Now you can explain every word in it. Quantized: the same photo saved as a smaller file. Mixture of experts: a hospital where you only see the specialists you need. Context window: the AI's desk.

That was the whole idea of this series: every AI word has a hello world, a tiny example that makes it click.

## Your AI dictionary

- **Tokens** (2): An AI doesn't read words. It reads pieces, called tokens.
- **How an LLM writes** (3): An AI writes by guessing the next word, again and again.
- **Embeddings** (4): An embedding is a word's address on a map of meaning.
- **Attention** (5): You glanced back at the other words. So does an AI. That's attention.
- **Context window** (6): An AI's context window is its desk. When it's full, something falls off.
- **Temperature** (7): Temperature is how adventurous the AI's next word is.
- **Parameters** (8): 70B means 70 billion tiny knobs, each tuned during training.
- **Training vs inference** (9): Training is school. Chatting is the exam. The AI isn't learning mid-exam.
- **Pretraining, fine-tuning and LoRA** (10): Pretraining is chef school. Fine-tuning is a short course. LoRA is a recipe card.
- **RLHF** (11): Thumbs up, thumbs down, thousands of times. That's how AIs learned to answer.
- **Distillation** (12): What if the big one taught the small one? That's distillation.
- **Synthetic data** (13): So an AI writes them, and another AI learns from them. That's synthetic data.
- **Scaling laws** (14): AI improved the same way. Researchers call it scaling laws.
- **Quantization** (15): Quantization is saving the same photo as a smaller file.
- **Mixture of experts** (16): A mixture of experts sends each word to only a few specialists.
- **KV cache** (17): Neither does an AI. It keeps notes, called the KV cache.
- **Speculative decoding** (18): That's speculative decoding: guess ahead, check in one go.
- **Why AI needs GPUs** (19): AI picks the students. That's why it runs on GPUs.
- **Small and on-device models** (20): On-device AI is the pocket dictionary.
- **Reasoning models** (21): Most people blurt ₹10. Thinking step by step gets ₹5. Reasoning models do the same.
- **Multimodal AI** (22): One AI that reads, sees and listens. That's multimodal.
- **Diffusion** (23): Now run it backwards. That's how AI draws: diffusion.
- **Voice AI** (24): New voice AI hears, thinks and speaks in one breath.
- **Open-weight vs closed models** (25): That's the difference between closed and open-weight AI models.
- **A2A (agent-to-agent)** (26): A2A is how AI agents swap business cards and hand over tasks.
- **Computer use** (27): Look, decide, click, look again. That's computer use.
- **World models** (28): A world model is an AI that imagines what happens next.
- **Benchmarks** (29): A benchmark is a school exam for AI. Your work is a different exam.
- **Hallucination** (30): An AI guesses likely words. Likely isn't always true. That's a hallucination.
- **AI watermarking** (31): AI watermarks work the same way: invisible, until you check.
- **Vibe coding** (32): Describe it, test it, ask for changes. That's vibe coding.

## How to learn the next AI word

1. Find the smallest example that shows it working.
2. Add one step to get to the real thing.
3. Find it in a product you already use.
4. Only then learn the technical name.
