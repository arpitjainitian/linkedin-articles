# Lesson 23.118: AI slop (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain AI slop with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

A buffet where someone has quietly added hundreds of trays of bland, reheated food. Technically it's food. You just have to dig to find the good dishes.

## One step further

Generative AI (write-up 6) makes content almost free to produce. Some people use that to churn out endless posts, fake product reviews, filler articles and surreal images aimed at algorithms.

The result: more noise for readers, harder searching, and a risk for future AI trained on it (write-up 46). Platforms respond with labels, ranking changes and detection.

## Exercise

Explain AI slop to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for AI slop? *That's AI slop: content made cheaply, for clicks, not for you.*
2. What is it not? *Not all AI-assisted content is slop. The difference is effort and value: a person with something to say, using AI as a tool, isn't producing slop.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *Odd AI images in social feeds, generic "top 10" articles with no real author, and AI-written comments that say nothing.*

## Glossary

- **Slop:** low-quality, mass-produced AI content.
- **Content farm:** a site churning out articles for ad clicks.
- **Signal-to-noise:** how much useful content there is among the junk.
