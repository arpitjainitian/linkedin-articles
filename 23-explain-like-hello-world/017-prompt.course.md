# Lesson 23.17: Prompt (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain prompt with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

Ask a new colleague "Write something about our product." You'll get something vague. Ask "Write a 3-line email to a busy CFO explaining how our product cuts invoice processing time, friendly tone." You'll get something useful.

## One step further

Remember write-up 11: an AI continues text in the most likely way. Your prompt is the start of that text. A clear prompt makes the right answer the most likely continuation.

Good prompts usually say who it's for, what you want, the format, and any examples or limits. Pasted documents, earlier chat messages and hidden instructions are part of the prompt too.

## Exercise

Explain prompt to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for prompt? *A prompt is your brief to the AI. Vague brief, vague answer.*
2. What is it not? *A prompt isn't a magic spell or a secret code. It's a clear brief. And a great prompt can't fix missing information; if the AI doesn't have the facts, give them.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *Every message you type into a chatbot. The text box in image generators.*

## Glossary

- **Prompt:** the input you give an AI.
- **Instruction:** the part that says what to do.
- **Output:** what the AI gives back.
