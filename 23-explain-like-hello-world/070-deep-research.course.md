# Lesson 23.70: Deep research (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain deep research with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

A junior analyst gets a question: "How big is the Indian EV charging market?" They search, read 30 pages, notice gaps, search again, take notes, and write a 5-page report with sources.

## One step further

Instead of one quick answer, the AI plans sub-questions, runs many web searches, reads the results, decides what's missing, searches again, and finally writes a structured report with citations (write-up 69). It can take minutes and hundreds of pages read.

It's great for a first draft of a research brief. It can still pick weak sources, miss paywalled data or misread numbers.

## Exercise

Explain deep research to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for deep research? *Deep research is an AI doing that whole loop on its own.*
2. What is it not? *Deep research isn't expert review. Treat the report like a junior analyst's first draft: useful, but check the key numbers and sources.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *"Deep research" or "research" modes in major AI chat apps.*

## Glossary

- **Research agent:** an AI that plans, searches and reads in a loop.
- **Sub-question:** a smaller question the main one is split into.
- **Report:** the final written summary with sources.
