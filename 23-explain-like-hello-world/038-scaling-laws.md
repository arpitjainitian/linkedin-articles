# 38. Why Did Bigger AI Keep Getting Better? | Scaling laws

![Cover](images/038-scaling-laws-cover.png)

*Explain It Like Hello World series: Scaling laws*

"Scaling laws." The reason companies spend billions on bigger AIs. And lately, the reason people ask if it's slowing down.

## The hello world

Practise piano for 1 hour and you improve a lot. 10 hours, you improve more. 100 hours, still more, but each extra hour adds a little less.

AI researchers found the same pattern: give a model more **data**, more **knobs** (write-up 26) and more **computing power**, and it gets predictably better. That's the hello world of **scaling laws**.

## One step further

The surprise was how predictable it was. Researchers could estimate how good a bigger model would be before building it, so companies kept scaling up.

But each step costs far more for a smaller gain, and good training text is limited. So the focus has shifted: better data, smarter training, and letting models think longer at answer time (write-up 71) instead of only getting bigger.

## Where you've already met it

News about record-sized AI data centres and chips. And headlines asking whether AI progress is hitting a wall.

## The real name

**Scaling laws**.

- **Scaling:** growing model size, data and compute together.
- **Compute:** the computing power used for training or answering.
- **Diminishing returns:** each extra unit helping a little less.

## What it's not

Scaling laws aren't laws of nature. They're observed trends that held for years, and they don't promise the trend continues forever.

## Take this to your next meeting

1. Expect progress from smarter methods, not just bigger models.
2. Don't assume the biggest model is the best value for your task.
3. Read "it's slowing down" headlines as "the easy gains are used up".

More practice helps. Each extra hour helps a little less.
