# Lesson 23.25: Streaming (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain streaming with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

Order a three-course meal. Would you rather wait 30 minutes for everything at once, or get the starter in 2 minutes while the rest is cooked?

## One step further

An AI writes one token at a time (write-up 11). Without streaming, you'd stare at a spinner until the last token is done. With streaming, the first words arrive in about a second and you start reading.

The total time is about the same; it just feels much faster. The time until the first word appears is a key speed measure (write-up 52).

## Exercise

Explain streaming to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for streaming? *Streaming serves each word of the answer as soon as it's ready.*
2. What is it not? *Streaming doesn't make the AI think faster, and it isn't typing for show. It's real: you're seeing the answer as it's written.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *Every chatbot that "types" its reply. Voice assistants that start speaking before they've finished thinking.*

## Glossary

- **Streaming:** sending output piece by piece as it's made.
- **Time to first token:** how long until the first words appear.
- **Server-sent events:** a common web technique for streaming.
