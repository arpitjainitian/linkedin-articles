# 25. What Does "Open" Mean in Open AI Models? | Open-weight vs closed models

![Cover](images/25-open-weight-cover.png)

*Explain It Like Hello World series: Open-weight vs closed models*

"Open-source AI." "Open-weight model." "Closed model." The words get mixed up in every debate. They don't mean the same thing.

## The hello world

A restaurant dish: you can eat it, but only at the restaurant, and you never see the recipe. That's a **closed** model, used through an app or API.

A recipe you take home: you cook it in your own kitchen, adjust it, and keep it. That's an **open-weight** model: you download its knobs (write-up 8) and run it yourself.

## One step further

"Open weights" means the trained model file is shared, often with a licence that has conditions. It usually doesn't mean the training data or full training method is shared; that would be fully "open source" in the strict sense, which is rarer.

Open-weight models give control, privacy and no per-question fee, but you run the servers. Closed models are easier and often more capable, but you depend on the provider.

## Where you've already met it

Model families like Llama, Mistral, Qwen or DeepSeek that you can download, versus services you can only use through an API.

## The real name

**Open-weight** versus **closed** models.

- **Weights:** the trained knobs; the model itself.
- **Licence:** the rules for how you may use the weights.
- **Self-hosting:** running the model on your own servers.

## What it's not

Open-weight isn't automatically "free for anything". Read the licence. And it isn't automatically safer or less safe; that depends on how you run it.

## Take this to your next meeting

1. Check the licence before building on an open-weight model.
2. Compare total cost: servers and people, not just the free download.
3. Use open weights where data must stay in your own systems.
