# Lesson 23.33: Fine-tuning (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain fine-tuning with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

A trained chef joins your restaurant. They can cook, but not your menu or your plating. So they spend two weeks cooking your dishes, corrected by your head chef.

## One step further

You collect examples of inputs and the outputs you want, often hundreds or thousands. The model continues training on them, and its knobs (write-up 28) shift towards your style.

Fine-tuning is good at teaching format, tone and repeatable tasks. It's weaker at adding fresh facts that change often; for those, looking them up at question time (write-up 58) usually works better.

## Exercise

Explain fine-tuning to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for fine-tuning? *Fine-tuning teaches a general AI your menu.*
2. What is it not? *Fine-tuning isn't the first fix to try. Better prompts and examples (write-up 23) cost nothing and often do the job.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *Company chatbots that answer in a consistent brand voice. Models specialised for medical notes or legal drafting.*

## Glossary

- **Fine-tuning:** extra training on focused examples.
- **Training examples:** input and ideal-output pairs.
- **Instruction tuning:** fine-tuning to follow instructions well.
