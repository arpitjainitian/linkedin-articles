# Lesson 23.76: Deepfake (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain deepfake with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

Take a video of an actor speaking. Replace their face, frame by frame, with someone else's face, matching every expression. Now it looks like the other person said it.

## One step further

The AI learns what a person's face and voice look and sound like from photos and recordings, then generates new footage in their likeness. Tools that once needed experts now run in apps.

That's why scams have shifted: fake video calls from a "CFO" asking for urgent transfers, fake celebrity endorsements, and fake evidence.

## Exercise

Explain deepfake to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for deepfake? *It might not be your CFO. That's a deepfake.*
2. What is it not? *Not every edited video is a deepfake, and not every deepfake is harmful (films use the technology with consent). The problem is deception without consent.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *News of video-call scams, fake endorsement ads, and election misinformation clips.*

## Glossary

- **Deepfake:** realistic fake media of a real person.
- **Face swap:** replacing one face with another in video.
- **Synthetic media:** AI-generated images, audio or video.
