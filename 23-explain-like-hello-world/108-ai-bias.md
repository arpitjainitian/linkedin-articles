# 108. What Is AI Bias, and How Does an AI End Up Unfair? | Bias

![Cover](images/108-ai-bias-cover.png)

*Explain It Like Hello World series: Bias*

"AI bias." Stories of AI that treated people unfairly by gender, race or region keep making news.

## The hello world

A hiring AI learns from 10 years of past hiring decisions. If those decisions mostly picked men for engineering roles, the AI learns "men suit engineering", and repeats it.

That's the hello world: **AI bias** is unfair patterns in an AI's outputs, usually learned from unfair patterns in its data.

## One step further

Bias can come from the data (some groups under-represented or treated unfairly in the past), the labels (write-up 42), the question asked, or how results are used. An image generator asked for "a CEO" may mostly draw men; a model may understand some accents worse.

Fixing it means measuring outcomes for different groups, improving data, and keeping people accountable for decisions.

## Where you've already met it

News of biased hiring tools, face recognition that works worse for darker skin, and image generators showing stereotypes.

## The real name

**AI bias**, and **fairness**.

- **Bias:** systematic unfairness in outputs.
- **Fairness testing:** comparing results across groups.
- **Representation:** how well each group appears in the data.

## What it's not

Bias isn't always intentional, and "the computer decided" isn't an excuse. The organisation using the AI is responsible for its decisions.

## Take this to your next meeting

1. Test outcomes across groups before using AI for decisions about people.
2. Ask vendors how they measured bias.
3. Keep a person accountable for high-stakes decisions.

Unfair data in, unfair decisions out.
