# Lesson 23.58: RAG (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain RAG with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

An open-book exam. The student doesn't need to remember the whole textbook. They find the right page, read it, then write the answer.

## One step further

Your documents are split into chunks and stored for search, often using embeddings (write-up 13) in a vector database (write-up 60). When a question comes in, the system finds the most relevant chunks and puts them on the AI's desk (write-up 16) with the question. The AI answers from what it was given, ideally with citations (write-up 66).

The answer can only be as good as what was found. If retrieval picks the wrong page, the AI answers confidently from the wrong page.

## Exercise

Explain RAG to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for RAG? *RAG is an AI doing an open-book exam on your documents.*
2. What is it not? *RAG isn't retraining the AI on your documents, and it isn't "plug in and done". Why it takes months to get right is the whole of my #JustAddRAG series.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *"Ask your documents" features, company help chatbots, AI search that shows sources.*

## Glossary

- **Retrieval:** finding the relevant pieces of information.
- **Chunk:** a small piece of a document stored for search.
- **Generation:** the AI writing the answer.
