# Lesson 23.111: Watermarking (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain watermarking with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

Banknotes have a watermark: hold one up to the light and a hidden mark appears. Invisible normally, checkable when needed.

## One step further

For images and audio, the signal is hidden in tiny changes people can't see or hear. For text, the AI slightly prefers certain word choices in a pattern a detector can spot. Separately, "content credentials" attach a signed label saying how a file was made, like a receipt.

Google's SynthID and the C2PA content credentials standard are two well-known examples.

## Exercise

Explain watermarking to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for watermarking? *AI watermarks work the same way: invisible, until you check.*
2. What is it not? *Watermarks aren't proof. Edits, cropping, re-typing or translating can weaken them, and "AI detectors" that guess from writing style are unreliable. No mark doesn't mean human-made.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *"AI-generated" labels on social media posts. Photo apps showing an info tag about how an image was made or edited.*

## Glossary

- **Watermark:** a hidden, detectable signal in content.
- **Provenance:** the record of where content came from.
- **Detector:** a tool that checks for the signal.
