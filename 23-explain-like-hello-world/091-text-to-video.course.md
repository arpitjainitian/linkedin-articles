# Lesson 23.91: Text-to-video (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain text-to-video with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

A flipbook: draw a slightly different picture on every page, flick through, and it moves.

## One step further

Most video generators extend diffusion (write-up 89): start from noise and clean it step by step, but for many frames at once, so they stay consistent. The hard part is keeping things the same across frames: the same face, the same shirt, objects that don't melt or vanish.

They learn from huge amounts of video, which is why they also inherit questions about consent and copyright.

## Exercise

Explain text-to-video to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for text-to-video? *Text-to-video AI draws that flipbook from a single sentence.*
2. What is it not? *It isn't filming anything real. Watch for physics slips: hands merging, objects appearing, impossible motion. And it isn't automatically free to use commercially; check the tool's terms.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *AI video tools from major tech companies, ads and social media clips made with AI, and short "AI films".*

## Glossary

- **Frame:** one still image in a video.
- **Temporal consistency:** keeping things the same from frame to frame.
- **Video diffusion:** diffusion applied across many frames.
