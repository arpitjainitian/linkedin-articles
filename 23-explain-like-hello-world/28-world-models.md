*Explain It Like Hello World series: World models*

"World models." Some researchers call them the next big step after chatbots. What's a "world" to an AI?

## The hello world

Throw a ball in your head. You can picture where it lands before it does. You have a little simulator of the world inside you.

That's the hello world: a **world model** is an AI that predicts what happens next in a scene: how things move, fall and react.

## One step further

Language models predict the next word. World models predict the next moment: the next video frame, or how a scene changes if a robot pushes a box.

They learn from huge amounts of video and interaction. Uses include training robots and self-driving systems in imagined worlds before the real one, and generating interactive game-like scenes.

## Where you've already met it

AI-generated videos where objects move realistically (and sometimes don't). Research demos of playable worlds generated by AI.

## The real name

**World models**.

- **Simulation:** imitating how the world behaves.
- **Embodied AI:** AI that acts in the physical world, like robots.
- **Physics:** how objects move, fall and collide.

## What it's not

World models don't truly understand physics yet. Watch AI videos closely: hands merge, objects vanish, glasses refill themselves.

## Take this to your next meeting

1. Watch this space for robotics and self-driving news.
2. Look for physics mistakes in AI video before trusting it.
3. Separate demos from products; many are early research.
