# Lesson 23.40: Data labelling (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain data labelling with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

Show a computer 10,000 photos. Someone has to mark each one: "cat", "dog", "neither". Without those marks, the computer can't learn which is which (write-up 3).

## One step further

Labels can be simple ("spam") or detailed: drawing boxes around cars, transcribing speech, rating which of two chatbot answers is better (write-up 36). Big AI projects use thousands of labellers, often through specialist companies.

Label quality is model quality. Inconsistent or biased labels teach the AI inconsistent or biased behaviour.

## Exercise

Explain data labelling to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for data labelling? *Data labelling: people marking the right answers so AI can learn.*
2. What is it not? *Labelling isn't unskilled busywork. Medical, legal and safety labels need experts, and clear guidelines matter as much as the people.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *"Select all squares with traffic lights" puzzles, which helped label images. Thumbs up and down buttons in chat apps.*

## Glossary

- **Label:** the right answer attached to an example.
- **Annotator:** the person adding labels.
- **Gold set:** a carefully checked set of labels used for testing.
