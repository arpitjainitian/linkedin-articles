# Lesson 23.23: Few-shot (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain few-shot with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

You want product names turned into short codes. Instead of explaining the rules, you show:
"Blue Cotton Shirt → BLU-SHT"
"Red Wool Scarf → RED-SCF"
"Green Linen Trousers → ?"

## One step further

Examples show format, tone and edge cases faster than words can describe them. Two to five good examples usually do the job.

Choose them carefully: the AI copies everything, including mistakes and accidental patterns. If all examples are short, answers will be short.

## Exercise

Explain few-shot to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for few-shot? *The AI answers GRN-TRS. That's few-shot: show a few examples, it copies the pattern.*
2. What is it not? *Few-shot doesn't retrain or change the model. It only guides this one answer. Remove the examples, and the effect is gone.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *Prompt templates that include "Example 1, Example 2". Spreadsheet AI features that learn from the first few rows you fill in.*

## Glossary

- **Few-shot:** a handful of examples in the prompt.
- **In-context learning:** learning a pattern from the prompt, without retraining.
- **Edge case:** an unusual example worth showing.
