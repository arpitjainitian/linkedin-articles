# Lesson 23.79: Digital twin (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain digital twin with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

A model railway that copies a real railway exactly, and updates live: every real train's position shows on the model instantly. Now you can test "what if we add a train?" on the model first.

## One step further

Sensors feed real data into the twin: a factory machine's temperature, a building's energy use, a city's traffic. Engineers simulate changes on the twin before trying them in reality. AI helps predict failures and suggest fixes.

The term is now also used for AI copies of people that answer like them, such as AI "twins" for hiring. Same idea: a stand-in you can ask questions.

## Exercise

Explain digital twin to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for digital twin? *A digital twin is a live copy you can safely experiment on.*
2. What is it not? *A twin is only as good as its data. An outdated or simplified twin can give confident wrong answers about the real thing.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *Manufacturing and city-planning dashboards, and the AI twins that recruiters can question (my Today in AI No. 011 covers one).*

## Glossary

- **Digital twin:** a live virtual copy of a real thing.
- **Sensor:** a device feeding real-world data in.
- **Simulation:** testing changes on the copy first.
