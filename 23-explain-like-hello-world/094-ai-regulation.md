# 94. Who Makes the Rules for AI? | AI regulation

![Cover](images/094-ai-regulation-cover.png)

*Explain It Like Hello World series: AI regulation*

"The EU AI Act." "AI regulation." Laws about AI are arriving, and every company using AI has to pay attention.

## The hello world

Toys, cars and medicines all have safety rules, stricter for riskier products. A toy car needs less paperwork than a real one.

That's the hello world: **AI regulation** sets rules for AI based on how much harm it could do: light rules for low-risk uses, strict ones for high-risk uses, bans for a few.

## One step further

The EU AI Act is the best-known example. It sorts AI uses into risk levels: some uses are banned (like social scoring by governments), high-risk uses (like AI in hiring, credit or medical devices) need testing, documentation and human oversight, and some uses need transparency, like telling people they're talking to an AI. Its rules apply in stages over several years.

Other countries take different approaches, from voluntary guidelines to sector rules, and data protection laws (like India's DPDP Act) also apply to AI.

## Where you've already met it

Notices saying "you are chatting with an AI". Company AI policies and new compliance roles.

## The real name

**AI regulation**, such as the **EU AI Act**.

- **Risk-based regulation:** stricter rules for riskier uses.
- **High-risk AI:** uses that can seriously affect people's lives or rights.
- **Transparency obligation:** a duty to tell people AI is involved.

## What it's not

Regulation isn't only for AI builders. Companies using AI, especially for decisions about people, have duties too. (This is general information, not legal advice.)

## Take this to your next meeting

1. List where your organisation uses AI and how risky each use is.
2. Tell people when they're dealing with an AI.
3. Ask legal early for hiring, credit, health or safety uses.

Stricter rules for riskier uses.
