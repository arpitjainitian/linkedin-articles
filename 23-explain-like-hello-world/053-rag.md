# 53. How Does an AI Answer From Your Documents? | RAG

![Cover](images/053-rag-cover.png)

*Explain It Like Hello World series: RAG*

"Just add RAG." It's the most common way companies make AI answer from their own documents. The acronym hides a simple idea.

## The hello world

An open-book exam. The student doesn't need to remember the whole textbook. They find the right page, read it, then write the answer.

That's the hello world: **RAG** means the AI first **retrieves** the relevant pages from your documents, then **generates** its answer from them.

## One step further

Your documents are split into chunks and stored for search, often using embeddings (write-up 12) in a vector database (write-up 54). When a question comes in, the system finds the most relevant chunks and puts them on the AI's desk (write-up 15) with the question. The AI answers from what it was given, ideally with citations (write-up 59).

The answer can only be as good as what was found. If retrieval picks the wrong page, the AI answers confidently from the wrong page.

## Where you've already met it

"Ask your documents" features, company help chatbots, AI search that shows sources.

## The real name

**RAG**, retrieval-augmented generation.

- **Retrieval:** finding the relevant pieces of information.
- **Chunk:** a small piece of a document stored for search.
- **Generation:** the AI writing the answer.

## What it's not

RAG isn't retraining the AI on your documents, and it isn't "plug in and done". Why it takes months to get right is the whole of my #JustAddRAG series.

## Take this to your next meeting

1. Picture an open-book exam: finding the right page is half the job.
2. Check which sources an answer used.
3. Read #JustAddRAG before promising "just add RAG" in two weeks.

Find the right page. Then answer.
