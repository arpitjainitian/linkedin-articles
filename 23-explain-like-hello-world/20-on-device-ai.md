# 20. Can an AI Live on Your Phone? | Small and on-device models

![Cover](images/20-on-device-ai-cover.png)

*Explain It Like Hello World series: Small and on-device models*

"On-device AI." Phone makers now advertise AI that runs without the internet. How does something that needed data centres fit in your pocket?

## The hello world

A library has every book, but you have to travel there. A pocket dictionary is small, always with you, and fine for most quick look-ups.

That's the hello world: a **small language model** on your phone is the pocket dictionary; the big cloud AI is the library.

## One step further

Small models have a few billion knobs (write-up 8) instead of hundreds of billions. They're made capable through good data and distillation (write-up 12), and shrunk further with quantization (write-up 15). Phones now include chips built for this.

The benefits: works offline, answers instantly, and your data can stay on the phone. The trade-off: less knowledge and weaker reasoning, so many phones send hard questions to the cloud.

## Where you've already met it

Phone features that summarise notifications, clean up photos or transcribe calls without an internet connection.

## The real name

**Small language models (SLMs)** and **on-device AI**.

- **SLM:** a language model small enough for phones or laptops.
- **On-device:** running on your own device, not in the cloud.
- **Edge AI:** AI running close to where data is created.

## What it's not

On-device doesn't always mean private. Check whether a feature quietly hands harder requests to the cloud.

## Take this to your next meeting

1. Use on-device AI for quick, private, everyday tasks.
2. Check which features stay on the phone and which go to the cloud.
3. Expect a mix: pocket dictionary first, library when needed.

A pocket dictionary for quick look-ups, the library for the hard ones.
