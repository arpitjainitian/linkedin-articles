# Lesson 23.90: Voice AI (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain voice AI with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

Old voice assistants were a relay race: one system turned your speech into text, another wrote an answer, a third read it aloud. Each hand-off added a pause.

## One step further

The relay approach (speech-to-text, then text AI, then text-to-speech) still works and is easy to control. But it loses tone, like a sarcastic "great", and adds delay.

Speech-to-speech models turn audio into tokens (write-up 11) directly, reason over them, and produce audio tokens back. They can hear emotion, pause naturally and stop when you talk over them.

## Exercise

Explain voice AI to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for voice AI? *New voice AI hears, thinks and speaks in one breath.*
2. What is it not? *A natural voice isn't a sign the AI is more correct. And a human-sounding voice should still tell people they're talking to an AI.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *Voice modes in chat apps that let you interrupt mid-sentence. Customer service bots that sound surprisingly human.*

## Glossary

- **ASR:** automatic speech recognition: speech to text.
- **TTS:** text-to-speech: text to spoken audio.
- **Latency:** the delay before the reply starts.
