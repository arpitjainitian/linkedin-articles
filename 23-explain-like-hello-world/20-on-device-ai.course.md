# Lesson 23.20: Small and on-device models (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain small and on-device models with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

A library has every book, but you have to travel there. A pocket dictionary is small, always with you, and fine for most quick look-ups.

## One step further

Small models have a few billion knobs (write-up 8) instead of hundreds of billions. They're made capable through good data and distillation (write-up 12), and shrunk further with quantization (write-up 15). Phones now include chips built for this.

The benefits: works offline, answers instantly, and your data can stay on the phone. The trade-off: less knowledge and weaker reasoning, so many phones send hard questions to the cloud.

## Exercise

Explain small and on-device models to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for small and on-device models? *On-device AI is the pocket dictionary.*
2. What is it not? *On-device doesn't always mean private. Check whether a feature quietly hands harder requests to the cloud.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *Phone features that summarise notifications, clean up photos or transcribe calls without an internet connection.*

## Glossary

- **SLM:** a language model small enough for phones or laptops.
- **On-device:** running on your own device, not in the cloud.
- **Edge AI:** AI running close to where data is created.
