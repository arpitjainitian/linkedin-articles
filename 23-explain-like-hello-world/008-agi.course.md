# Lesson 23.8: AGI (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain AGI with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

A calculator beats you at sums. A chess program beats you at chess. But neither can also cook, learn a new language and fix a bike.

## One step further

Today's AI is impressive but uneven: brilliant at writing code, then confused by a simple puzzle. AGI would be broad and reliable across tasks, including new ones it wasn't trained for.

There's no agreed test. Some define it by economic value ("does most work people do"), others by learning ability. That's why predictions range from "a few years" to "never".

## Exercise

Explain AGI to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for AGI? *AGI means one AI that can learn most tasks a person can.*
2. What is it not? *AGI isn't the same as consciousness, and it isn't a product launch date. When someone says "AGI", ask which definition they mean.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *Headlines and company mission statements. Not yet in a product.*

## Glossary

- **Narrow AI:** good at specific tasks.
- **General intelligence:** able to learn and do a wide range of tasks.
- **Benchmark:** a test used to measure progress (write-up 97).
