# Lesson 23.4: Deep learning (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain deep learning with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

How do you recognise a cat? You don't think "pointed ears AND whiskers AND fur". You see edges, then shapes, then ears and eyes, then "cat", in layers, without trying.

## One step further

Each layer is part of a neural network (write-up 5). Early layers learn simple things like edges or sounds. Later layers combine them into faces, words or meanings.

It needs lots of data and computing power, which is why it took off around 2012, when GPUs (write-up 51) and huge image collections met. Today's LLMs are deep learning at enormous scale.

## Exercise

Explain deep learning to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for deep learning? *Deep learning learns in layers, just like that.*
2. What is it not? *"Deep" doesn't mean deep understanding. It means many layers. And it's not always the right tool; for small tables of numbers, simpler machine learning often wins.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *Photo apps that find "beach" pictures, speech-to-text, real-time translation, every chatbot.*

## Glossary

- **Layer:** one stage of pattern-spotting in the network.
- **Deep:** many layers stacked together.
- **Feature:** a pattern a layer has learned to detect.
