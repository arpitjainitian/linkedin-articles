# Lesson 23.32: Supervised learning (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain supervised learning with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

Learning with a teacher: flashcards with the answer on the back. Learning without one: a pile of photos you sort into groups yourself, because some just look alike.

## One step further

Supervised learning needs labelled data (write-up 42): emails marked spam or not, X-rays marked by doctors. It then predicts answers for new examples. Unsupervised learning groups customers by behaviour or spots unusual transactions, with no labels at all.

LLMs use a third trick, self-supervised learning: hide the next word and guess it, so the text itself provides the answers (write-up 34).

## Exercise

Explain supervised learning to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for supervised learning? *That's supervised versus unsupervised learning: with answers, or without.*
2. What is it not? *Unsupervised doesn't mean no humans involved. People still choose the data, check the groups and decide what they mean.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *Spam filters (supervised), "customers like you" groupings and many fraud alerts (often unsupervised).*

## Glossary

- **Label:** the right answer attached to an example.
- **Clustering:** grouping similar things without labels.
- **Self-supervised:** the data provides its own answers.
