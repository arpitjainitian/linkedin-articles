# Lesson 23.62: Agentic AI (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain agentic AI with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

Think of a scale, not a switch. At one end: a calculator, which does exactly what you press. At the other: a trusted manager, who decides what to do and does it.

## One step further

A system can be a little agentic (an AI picks which of two tools to call) or very agentic (it plans a multi-day project and acts without asking). "Agentic AI" usually means systems towards the freer end, built around agents (write-up 61).

More freedom means more flexibility, but also less predictability, more cost and more need for limits and checks.

## Exercise

Explain agentic AI to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for agentic AI? *Agentic is how far along that scale an AI system sits.*
2. What is it not? *"Agentic" isn't a quality badge. More autonomy isn't always better; for routine work, a fixed workflow is often cheaper and safer (Series 2, write-up 6).*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *Product launches promising "agentic" features, from email to coding to customer service.*

## Glossary

- **Agentic:** acting with some independence towards a goal.
- **Autonomy:** how much it decides without asking.
- **Human in the loop:** a person approving key steps.
