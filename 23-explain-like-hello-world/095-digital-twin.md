# 95. What's a Digital Twin, and Why Do Factories Want One? | Digital twin

![Cover](images/095-digital-twin-cover.png)

*Explain It Like Hello World series: Digital twin*

"Digital twin." Factories, cities and hospitals talk about them. Now the term is used for AI copies of people too.

## The hello world

A model railway that copies a real railway exactly, and updates live: every real train's position shows on the model instantly. Now you can test "what if we add a train?" on the model first.

That's the hello world: a **digital twin** is a live virtual copy of something real, used to monitor it and test changes safely.

## One step further

Sensors feed real data into the twin: a factory machine's temperature, a building's energy use, a city's traffic. Engineers simulate changes on the twin before trying them in reality. AI helps predict failures and suggest fixes.

The term is now also used for AI copies of people that answer like them, such as AI "twins" for hiring. Same idea: a stand-in you can ask questions.

## Where you've already met it

Manufacturing and city-planning dashboards, and the AI twins that recruiters can question (my Today in AI No. 012 covers one).

## The real name

A **digital twin**.

- **Digital twin:** a live virtual copy of a real thing.
- **Sensor:** a device feeding real-world data in.
- **Simulation:** testing changes on the copy first.

## What it's not

A twin is only as good as its data. An outdated or simplified twin can give confident wrong answers about the real thing.

## Take this to your next meeting

1. Ask how often the twin's data updates.
2. Test predictions against reality regularly.
3. For person-twins, check consent and accuracy.

A live copy to test on before touching the real thing.
