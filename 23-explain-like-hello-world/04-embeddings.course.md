# Lesson 23.4: Embeddings (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain embeddings with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

Draw a map. Put "king" on it. Put "queen" right next to it. Put "banana" far away, near "apple".

## One step further

A real map has two directions: north and east. An AI's meaning map has hundreds or thousands of directions at once, so it can place things by many qualities: royalty, gender, food, formality.

Each position is just a long list of numbers. To ask "how similar are these two things?", the AI measures how close their positions are. It works for sentences, pages and images too, not just single words.

## Exercise

Explain embeddings to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for embeddings? *An embedding is a word's address on a map of meaning.*
2. What is it not? *An embedding isn't a definition or a keyword. Two texts can share no words and still sit close together. That's the point. (How this powers document search is covered in #JustAddRAG.)*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *Searching "cheap flights" and finding "budget airfares". Spotify suggesting songs that feel similar. Photo apps finding "dog" pictures you never labelled.*

## Glossary

- **Embedding:** a list of numbers that places something on a map of meaning.
- **Vector:** that list of numbers.
- **Similarity search:** finding the items closest on the map.
