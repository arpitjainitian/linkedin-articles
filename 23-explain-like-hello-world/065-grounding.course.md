# Lesson 23.65: Grounding (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain grounding with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

Two students answer "When was the company founded?" One answers from memory, confidently. The other opens the annual report, finds the line, and answers from it.

## One step further

The source is put on the AI's desk (write-up 16), from documents (write-up 58), a database, or a web search. The AI is instructed to answer only from it and to say so when the source doesn't contain the answer.

Grounding reduces hallucinations (write-up 92) a lot, but doesn't remove them: the AI can still misread or overstate the source. Citations (write-up 66) let people check.

## Exercise

Explain grounding to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for grounding? *Grounding makes the AI the second student.*
2. What is it not? *Grounded isn't the same as correct. If the source is out of date or wrong, a grounded answer is too.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *AI answers that say "according to your document" or show search results they used.*

## Glossary

- **Grounding:** basing answers on a provided source.
- **Source:** the trusted text the answer comes from.
- **Faithfulness:** how closely the answer sticks to the source.
