# Lesson 23.69: Citations (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain citations with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

A good research report puts a small number after each claim: [1], [2]. At the bottom, [1] tells you exactly which source said it.

## One step further

When an AI answers from given documents (write-ups 60 and 68), the system can label each source and ask the AI to mark which one supports each claim. Some systems quote the exact sentence.

Without given sources, an AI asked to "add references" may invent plausible ones (write-up 99). Real citations come from real retrieved sources, not from memory.

## Exercise

Explain citations to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for citations? *Citations give every AI answer a receipt.*
2. What is it not? *A citation isn't proof the claim is right. Click it: the source may not actually say that.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *AI search tools with numbered source links. Document chatbots that highlight the passage they used.*

## Glossary

- **Citation:** a pointer to the source behind a claim.
- **Attribution:** linking an answer to where it came from.
- **Quote:** the exact source text, shown alongside.
