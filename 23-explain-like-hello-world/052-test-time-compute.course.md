# Lesson 23.52: Test-time compute (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain test-time compute with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

A quiz. Answer instantly, or take five minutes on scrap paper. The second takes longer, and on hard questions it's right more often.

## One step further

Older models got smarter mostly by training bigger (write-up 44). Reasoning models (write-up 86) also get better by thinking longer at answer time: writing working steps, checking them, trying again.

Many products let you set a thinking budget: low for quick replies, high for hard problems. More thinking means more tokens, so more cost and a longer wait (write-up 58).

## Exercise

Explain test-time compute to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for test-time compute? *Test-time compute lets an AI use the scrap paper.*
2. What is it not? *More thinking isn't always better. On easy questions it just adds cost and delay. Match the budget to the task.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *A chatbot showing "thinking..." before it answers, and settings like "think harder" or "extended thinking".*

## Glossary

- **Test time:** when the model is being used, not trained.
- **Thinking budget:** how much the AI may think before answering.
- **Reasoning tokens:** the working steps, which you pay for even when hidden.
