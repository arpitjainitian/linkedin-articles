# 31. Can You Tell If AI Wrote This? | AI watermarking

![Cover](images/31-watermarking-cover.png)

*Explain It Like Hello World series: AI watermarking*

"AI watermarking." "Content credentials." With AI images and text everywhere, people want to know what's real. Can you actually tell?

## The hello world

Banknotes have a watermark: hold one up to the light and a hidden mark appears. Invisible normally, checkable when needed.

That's the hello world: an **AI watermark** is a hidden signal placed in AI-made images, audio or text that a checking tool can detect.

## One step further

For images and audio, the signal is hidden in tiny changes people can't see or hear. For text, the AI slightly prefers certain word choices in a pattern a detector can spot. Separately, "content credentials" attach a signed label saying how a file was made, like a receipt.

Google's SynthID and the C2PA content credentials standard are two well-known examples.

## Where you've already met it

"AI-generated" labels on social media posts. Photo apps showing an info tag about how an image was made or edited.

## The real name

**AI watermarking** and **content credentials** (provenance).

- **Watermark:** a hidden, detectable signal in content.
- **Provenance:** the record of where content came from.
- **Detector:** a tool that checks for the signal.

## What it's not

Watermarks aren't proof. Edits, cropping, re-typing or translating can weaken them, and "AI detectors" that guess from writing style are unreliable. No mark doesn't mean human-made.

## Take this to your next meeting

1. Treat "AI-generated" labels as helpful, not final.
2. Don't judge people's work with AI-writing detectors alone.
3. Add content credentials to your company's official images where you can.
