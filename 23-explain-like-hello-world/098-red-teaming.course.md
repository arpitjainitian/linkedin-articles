# Lesson 23.98: Red teaming (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain red teaming with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

Before a bank opens a new vault, it hires professional burglars to try to break in. Every way they get in becomes a fix.

## One step further

Red teamers try jailbreaks (write-up 96), prompt injection (write-up 97), requests for harmful information, bias (write-up 101), privacy leaks and misuse of tools. Some are human experts; some are AI systems generating thousands of attacks.

Every failure found becomes a test case (write-up 99) so it's checked again after every change.

## Exercise

Explain red teaming to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for red teaming? *Red teaming is attacking your own AI before anyone else does.*
2. What is it not? *Red teaming isn't a one-off box to tick before launch. New tricks appear constantly, so it's repeated, especially after changes.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *AI companies publishing red-team results in model "system cards". Government AI safety institutes testing models before release.*

## Glossary

- **Red team:** the people or tools attacking the system.
- **Blue team:** the people defending and fixing it.
- **Adversarial testing:** testing with deliberately tricky inputs.
