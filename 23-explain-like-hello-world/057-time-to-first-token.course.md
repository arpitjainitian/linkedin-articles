# Lesson 23.57: Time to first token (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain time to first token with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

At a restaurant, a waiter who brings bread and water in one minute feels fast, even if the meal takes 20. A waiter who brings nothing for 15 minutes feels slow, even if everything arrives at once.

## One step further

With streaming (write-up 27), people start reading as soon as the first word arrives. So the wait before that first word decides whether an AI feels fast.

It grows with long prompts, document look-ups before answering, reasoning time (write-up 79) and busy servers. Prompt caching (write-up 48) and smaller prompts shrink it.

## Exercise

Explain time to first token to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for time to first token? *Time to first token is the AI's bread basket.*
2. What is it not? *A fast first word doesn't mean a fast full answer. Measure both: the first word and the speed of the rest.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *Chatbots that start typing almost instantly, versus ones that think for 10 seconds first.*

## Glossary

- **TTFT:** the delay before the first word appears.
- **Tokens per second:** how fast words keep arriving after that.
- **Perceived speed:** how fast it feels to the user.
