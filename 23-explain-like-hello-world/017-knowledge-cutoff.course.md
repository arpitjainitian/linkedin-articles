# Lesson 23.17: Knowledge cutoff (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain knowledge cutoff with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

An encyclopedia printed last year. Everything in it was true when it went to print, but it knows nothing about what happened since.

## One step further

A model learns from a snapshot of text collected up to a certain date (write-up 32). Training takes months, so a model is often released well after its cutoff, and then used for a long time.

To know newer things, the AI needs fresh information at question time: web search, your documents (write-up 58), or tools (write-up 70). Without them, it may guess, and guesses can sound sure (write-up 92).

## Exercise

Explain knowledge cutoff to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for knowledge cutoff? *That's a knowledge cutoff: the date an AI stops knowing things on its own.*
2. What is it not? *It isn't a bug a quick update fixes. Retraining is expensive, so fresh facts usually come from search or documents, not from the model itself.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *A chatbot that says "as of my last update", or one that searches the web before answering about recent events.*

## Glossary

- **Cutoff date:** the last date covered by the training data.
- **Snapshot:** the fixed set of text a model learned from.
- **Web search:** fetching fresh pages at question time.
