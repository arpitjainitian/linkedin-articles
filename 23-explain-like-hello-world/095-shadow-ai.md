# 95. Is Your Team Using AI You Don't Know About? | Shadow AI

![Cover](images/095-shadow-ai-cover.png)

*Explain It Like Hello World series: Shadow AI*

"Shadow AI." Security teams worry about it more than about rogue robots. It's happening in most offices right now.

## The hello world

A company bans personal cloud storage. An employee, in a hurry, pastes a confidential contract into a free AI chatbot to summarise it. Nobody approved it, nobody knows, and the contract has left the building.

That's the hello world: **shadow AI** is staff using AI tools the organisation hasn't approved or doesn't know about.

## One step further

People use AI because it helps them work faster. If approved tools are missing or clumsy, they use personal accounts instead. The risks: confidential data sent to unknown services, outputs nobody checks, and no record of what was used.

Bans rarely work. Most organisations do better by offering good approved tools, clear rules on what data can go where, and simple guidance.

## Where you've already met it

Company emails reminding staff not to paste client data into public chatbots. Security tools that detect AI usage.

## The real name

**Shadow AI**.

- **Shadow IT:** technology used without official approval.
- **Data leakage:** confidential information leaving the organisation.
- **Acceptable use policy:** rules for what staff may use AI for.

## What it's not

Shadow AI isn't staff being malicious. It's usually people trying to work faster. The fix is better options, not just blame.

## Take this to your next meeting

1. Provide an approved AI tool that's actually good.
2. Publish simple rules: what data may go into which tools.
3. Ask teams what they use, without punishment, to find the gaps.

If the approved tool is missing, people find their own.
