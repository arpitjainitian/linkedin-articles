# Lesson 23.48: NPU (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain NPU with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

Your laptop has a main brain (CPU) for everything and a graphics chip for games. Now it also has a small, energy-saving helper for one kind of job: running AI features.

## One step further

Running AI on the main chip is slow and drains power. An NPU handles the number-crunching of neural networks (write-up 5) efficiently, so features like background blur, live captions or on-device assistants (write-up 50) can run constantly.

NPUs are small compared with data-centre chips, so they run small models (write-up 49), not the biggest ones.

## Exercise

Explain NPU to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for NPU? *That's the NPU: low-power AI, right on your device.*
2. What is it not? *An NPU won't run the largest AI models; those still need the cloud. And a big TOPS number doesn't guarantee useful features.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *"Copilot+ PC" and "AI PC" laptops, and phone chips with a "neural engine". Video-call background blur and live captions.*

## Glossary

- **NPU:** a power-efficient chip for running AI on devices.
- **TOPS:** trillions of operations per second, a common NPU speed figure.
- **On-device AI:** AI running locally (write-up 50).
