# Lesson 23.66: Knowledge graph (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain knowledge graph with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

Draw dots for people and companies. Draw a line from "Priya" to "Acme Ltd" labelled "works at", and from "Acme Ltd" to "Mumbai" labelled "based in".

## One step further

Instead of storing paragraphs, it stores relationships: who reports to whom, which product uses which part, which drug interacts with which. That makes questions like "Which suppliers of Acme are based in Mumbai?" easy to answer exactly, by following the links.

Building one takes work: someone (or an AI) has to extract the facts and keep them up to date. GraphRAG (write-up 67) combines knowledge graphs with AI answers.

## Exercise

Explain knowledge graph to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for knowledge graph? *A knowledge graph stores facts as things and the links between them.*
2. What is it not? *A knowledge graph isn't a chart you draw once. It's a living database that needs updating. And it's not needed for every AI project.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *Google's information panel on the right of search results. "People you may know" on social networks.*

## Glossary

- **Node:** a thing: a person, company or place.
- **Edge:** a labelled link between two things.
- **Triple:** one fact: thing, link, thing.
