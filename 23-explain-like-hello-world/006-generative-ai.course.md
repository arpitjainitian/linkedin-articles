# Lesson 23.6: Generative AI (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain generative AI with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

Old AI looks at a photo and says "cat". It **recognises**.

## One step further

It learns the patterns of huge amounts of existing content, then produces new content that fits those patterns. LLMs (write-up 12) generate text a token at a time; diffusion models (write-up 89) generate images from noise.

The output is new, not copied, but shaped entirely by what it learned from, which is why questions about quality, bias and copyright come with it.

## Exercise

Explain generative AI to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for generative AI? *Generative AI makes new things instead of just labelling old ones.*
2. What is it not? *Generative isn't "creative" the way people are, and it isn't a search engine. It produces likely-looking content, which can be wrong (write-up 99).*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini writing text; image generators; AI music tools; coding assistants.*

## Glossary

- **Generative:** creating new content.
- **Discriminative:** classifying or predicting a label.
- **Prompt:** the instruction that tells it what to create.
