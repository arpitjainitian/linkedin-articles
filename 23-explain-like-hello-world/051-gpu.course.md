# Lesson 23.51: GPU (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain GPU with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

You need to solve a million simple sums. One brilliant professor does them one at a time. A thousand students each do a thousand, all at once.

## One step further

Every token an AI reads or writes means multiplying huge grids of numbers, its knobs (write-up 28), many times over. Each sum is easy, but there are billions, and most can be done at the same time.

GPUs were designed to colour millions of screen pixels at once for games, which is exactly this kind of work. AI chips today add lots of fast memory too, because the model's knobs have to sit right next to the sums.

## Exercise

Explain GPU to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for GPU? *AI picks the students. That's why it runs on GPUs.*
2. What is it not? *A GPU isn't smarter than a CPU. It's built for many simple sums at once. Other AI chips (like Google's TPUs) use the same idea.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *News about Nvidia, chip shortages and AI data centres. Gaming PCs that can also run AI models locally.*

## Glossary

- **CPU:** a general-purpose chip, great at one complex task at a time.
- **GPU:** a chip with thousands of small cores doing simple sums in parallel.
- **Parallel computing:** doing many calculations at the same time.
