# Lesson 23.21: Prompt engineering (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain prompt engineering with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

One person asks the AI 50 questions and copies the answers that look fine. Another writes the instructions once, tests them on 50 real cases, fixes what fails, and saves the version that works.

## One step further

In real products, prompts are reused thousands of times. So teams write them carefully: clear role, steps, format, examples (write-up 24), and rules for unclear cases. Then they test them against a set of real examples (write-up 106), change one thing at a time, and keep versions.

As models get better at understanding plain requests, the tricks matter less and the clear thinking matters more.

## Exercise

Explain prompt engineering to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for prompt engineering? *Prompt engineering is the second person.*
2. What is it not? *It isn't finding magic words, and it can't make a model do what it's unable to do. For hard rules and guarantees, use code, not prompts (Series 3).*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *Any AI feature in an app you use: someone wrote and tested the prompt behind it.*

## Glossary

- **Prompt template:** a reusable prompt with blanks for each case.
- **Iteration:** changing one thing, then testing again.
- **Prompt version:** a saved, tested copy of a prompt.
