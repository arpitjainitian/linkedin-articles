# Lesson 23.22: Zero-shot (course companion)

*Explain It Like Hello World. Not for LinkedIn.*

## Learning objective

After this lesson, you can explain zero-shot with a hello-world example, say how it really works in one step, and name its most common misunderstanding.

## The hello world, to retell

Ask an AI: "Is this review positive or negative? 'The battery died in an hour.'" You gave no examples, no training, just the question. It answers "negative".

## One step further

Older AI needed thousands of labelled examples for each task (write-up 3). Large models learned so much general language that they can follow a plain instruction for many new tasks straight away.

Zero-shot works well for common, clear tasks. For unusual formats or subtle judgement calls, adding a few examples (write-up 23) usually helps.

## Exercise

Explain zero-shot to someone non-technical in under one minute, using your own everyday example (not the one from the article). Then name one product where they have already met it.

## Quiz

1. What is the hello-world example for zero-shot? *The AI answers "negative" anyway. That's zero-shot.*
2. What is it not? *Zero-shot doesn't mean zero knowledge. The model learned from huge amounts of text. And it's not always reliable for niche tasks.*
3. Name one place you've already met it. *Asking a chatbot to translate, classify or summarise something without showing it how.*

## Glossary

- **Shot:** an example included in the prompt.
- **Zero-shot:** no examples, just the instruction.
- **Generalisation:** handling tasks it wasn't specifically trained on.
