# Series 2: "Users will love the chatbot"

**Theme:** The AI can be accurate and still fail, because of how it feels to use.

**Level:** L1. Written so anyone can follow it, with no domain knowledge needed.

**Running example:** **Tony's Pizza order bot.** A small pizza shop adds a chatbot to take orders and answer questions. Everyone has ordered a pizza, so nobody needs to learn a domain.

**Hashtag:** #UsersWillLoveIt (proposal)

## Planned write-ups

| # | Title | The hook | What it covers | Example moment | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why Do Customers Try Your Chatbot Once and Never Come Back? | Launch week: 300 chats. Week three: a loyal 12. | Curtain raiser: the gap between "it works" and "people use it" | Customers try the bot once, then go back to phoning Tony | Planned |
| 2 | Your Chatbot Has 10 Seconds. What Does It Do With Them? | An empty box and a blinking cursor is the scariest screen in AI. | First impressions, suggested questions, saying upfront what it can and can't do | Customer types "hi". The bot replies with the entire menu | Planned |
| 3 | Why Does Your Chatbot Sound So Sure When It's Wrong? | A wrong answer said confidently costs more trust than a right one said nervously. | Confidence, tone, showing sources, the price of a polite wrong answer | "Yes, the Margherita is gluten-free!" It isn't | Planned |
| 4 | What Happens When the Customer Says "No, Not That"? | Real conversations go sideways. Most bots can't steer back. | Clarifying questions, fixing misunderstandings mid-chat, keeping context | "Large. No wait, make it two mediums. Actually, extra cheese on one." | Planned |
| 5 | Why Do Customers Keep Asking Your Bot Things It Can't Do? | Customers don't read the scope document. They just ask. | Setting expectations, graceful refusals, pointing somewhere useful | "Can I pay half now and half next week?" | Planned |

## Format (same as #JustAddRAG)

- Curiosity-question titles, numbered from 1, "write-up" naming, no fixed schedule.
- Start with why the topic matters; plain-language analogies with technical names revealed at the end; light "Fix:" pointers.
- Polite humor, one very simple running example, cheat sheet at the end, three kickoff takeaways.
- Post text in a copy-paste block, with the "you pay for it" impact of the wrong choice.
- Cover: its own iceberg plus an original meme scene and a sticky-note punchline.
