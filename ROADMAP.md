# Roadmap: AI engineering that looks simple, and fails often

Each topic is its own series, numbered below. Each is a sentence that sounds easy in a kickoff meeting and turns out to be the hard part of an AI project.

**Order:** technical difficulty goes up gradually, from L1 (easy) to L5 (advanced).
**Writing:** stays as simple as possible at every level: plain words, everyday analogies, real examples.

| Series | Topic ("looks simple") | Level | What it covers | Status | Why a full series, not one article |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Just add RAG over our docs" | L2 | A full series of write-ups, see [01-just-add-rag/](01-just-add-rag/README.md) | **In progress** | RAG hides 16 separate layers (ingestion, chunking, retrieval, permissions, cost...). Any one of them can sink the project on its own, and each needs its own explanation. |
| 2 | "Build it and they will chat" | L1 | A series of write-ups on trust, conversation design and the human side of AI. Example: A bank's customer-support chatbot. Plan: [02-users-will-love-the-chatbot/](02-users-will-love-the-chatbot/README.md) | **Planned (outline ready)** | Trust is won or lost across many different moments: the first 10 seconds, a wrong answer, a handover, a language mismatch, a complaint. Each moment needs its own design. |
| 3 | "We bought AI, so we're AI-first now" | L1 | Process redesign, success metrics. Example: An e-commerce company's 500 support agents getting AI. Plan: [03-ai-transformation/](03-ai-transformation/README.md) | **Planned (outline ready)** | Transformation touches people, process, skills, metrics, governance and culture. Buying licences is one decision; changing how work gets done is many. |
| 4 | "It worked in the demo" | L2 | The POC-to-production cliff, ownership. Example: Invoice processing in accounts payable. Plan: [04-poc-to-production/](04-poc-to-production/README.md) | **Planned (outline ready)** | The gap between demo and production is a dozen gaps, each owned by a different team: security, operations, support, finance, legal. |
| 5 | "What's a few cents between friends?" | L2 | Inference economics: tokens, caching, model choice, the total bill. Example: A product-question assistant on a shopping site. Plan: [05-few-cents-per-call/](05-few-cents-per-call/README.md) | **Planned (outline ready)** | An AI bill has many independent drivers: tokens, model choice, caching, retries, infrastructure, people. Each has its own trap and its own fix. |
| 6 | "Works on my prompt" | L2 | Evals: test cases, judge LLMs, rubrics, silent regressions. Example: An HR policy assistant for employees. Plan: [06-the-prompt-works-ship-it/](06-the-prompt-works-ship-it/README.md) | **Planned (outline ready)** | Testing AI is a discipline, not a step: test sets, answer keys, judge models, regressions, safety tests and live monitoring each deserve their own deep dive. |
| 7 | "Just ask it for JSON" | L3 | Structured output, validation. Example: Resume screening into candidate profiles. Plan: [07-llm-output-to-json/](07-llm-output-to-json/README.md) | **Planned (outline ready)** | Getting reliable structure out of a model touches formats, validation, retries, partial failures and changing schemas. Each fails in its own way. |
| 8 | "Just put the rules in the prompt" | L3 | Deterministic rules vs a probabilistic model. Example: An airline's refund and cancellation assistant. Plan: [08-business-rules/](08-business-rules/README.md) | **Planned (outline ready)** | Where code ends and the model begins differs for eligibility, pricing, compliance and approvals. One rule of thumb doesn't fit every rule. |
| 9 | "Just tell it not to" | L3 | Guardrails: input and output filters, PII, jailbreaks, policy. Example: An online pharmacy's medicine-question assistant. Plan: [09-guardrails/](09-guardrails/README.md) | **Planned (outline ready)** | Guardrails are layers, not a single prompt: input checks, output checks, personal data, jailbreaks and policy each stop a different failure. |
| 10 | "We'll know when it breaks" | L3 | Observability: traces, logs, metrics, feedback. Example: A food-delivery app's support bot. Plan: [10-observability/](10-observability/README.md) | **Planned (outline ready)** | Seeing inside an AI system needs traces, metrics, feedback, drift detection and cost tracking. Each answers a different 'why'. |
| 11 | "Everyone gets an API key" | L4 | AI gateway: auth, routing, rate limits, caching, fallbacks across providers. Example: A 5,000-person company sharing one AI platform. Plan: [11-ai-gateway/](11-ai-gateway/README.md) | **Planned (outline ready)** | A gateway handles access, routing, limits, caching, fallbacks and governance. Each solves a different problem when many teams share AI. |
| 12 | "Just connect the dots" | L4 | GraphRAG: entities and relationships, multi-hop questions, explainable paths, and the real cost of building and maintaining the graph. Example: Supplier risk at a manufacturer. Plan: [12-knowledge-graph/](12-knowledge-graph/README.md) | **Planned (outline ready)** | A knowledge graph has to be designed, built, kept current and queried, and it's not always worth it. Each step is a project of its own. |
| 13 | "Just plug it in" | L4 | What MCP is, why it helps, and what it doesn't solve. Example: An IT helpdesk assistant connected through MCP. Plan: [13-mcp-server/](13-mcp-server/README.md) | **Planned (outline ready)** | MCP looks like plug-and-play, but servers, discovery, permissions, versioning and third-party trust each raise different questions. |
| 14 | "Give the agent the keys" | L4 | Tool security, least privilege, prompt injection through data. Example: An e-commerce support agent that can issue refunds. Plan: [14-connect-agent-to-tools/](14-connect-agent-to-tools/README.md) | **Planned (outline ready)** | Every tool is a new door: databases, email, payments and files each carry different risks and need different controls. |
| 15 | "It should just remember me" | L4 | AI memory: short-term context, summarising long chats, long-term memory stores, what to remember and what to forget, privacy of remembered facts. Example: A shopping assistant that remembers you. Plan: [15-ai-memory/](15-ai-memory/README.md) | **Planned (outline ready)** | Memory spans short-term context, long-term stores, what to forget, privacy and cost. Getting one right doesn't fix the others. |
| 16 | "Just make it agentic" | L4 | Agentic loops: plan, act, observe, reflect; when a workflow beats an agent. Example: A corporate travel-booking agent. Plan: [16-lets-make-it-agentic/](16-lets-make-it-agentic/README.md) | **Planned (outline ready)** | An agent is a loop with many moving parts: planning, tools, stopping, recovery and testing. Each fails differently. |
| 17 | "Let it do its own research" | L5 | Agentic RAG: plan, retrieve, reason, retrieve again; loops, cost and latency, when plain RAG is enough. Example: Contract review for the legal team. Plan: [17-agentic-rag/](17-agentic-rag/README.md) | **Planned (outline ready)** | Letting the agent search adds loops, extra cost, extra latency and new failure modes, plus the question of when plain RAG is enough. |
| 18 | "More agents, more brains" | L5 | Multi-agent systems: orchestrators, isolated context, compounding errors. Example: New-employee onboarding with multiple agents. Plan: [18-multi-agent/](18-multi-agent/README.md) | **Planned (outline ready)** | Many agents bring orchestration patterns, handoffs, shared context and compounding errors. Each pattern needs its own trade-offs. |
| 19 | "We bought the brain, we're done" | L5 | The agent harness: you own the loop. Example: An insurance claims-processing agent. Plan: [19-agent-harness/](19-agent-harness/README.md) | **Planned (outline ready)** | The harness around the model (planner, memory, tools, state, guardrails, evals) is everything you own. Each part deserves its own look. |
