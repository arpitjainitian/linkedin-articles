# Series 10: "We'll just check the logs"

**Theme:** If you can't explain a wrong answer, you can't fix it.

**Level:** L3. Written so anyone can follow it, with no domain knowledge needed.

**Running example:** **A weather-answer bot.** "Do I need an umbrella today?" Simple answers, but when one is wrong, someone gets wet and wants to know why.

**Hashtag:** #SeeInsideYourAI (proposal)

## Planned write-ups

| # | Title | The hook | What it covers | Example moment | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | It Said "No Umbrella". It Rained. Can You Tell Why? | "The AI was wrong" isn't a diagnosis. | Curtain raiser: observability for AI | Wrong city, old forecast, or a bad prompt? | Planned |
| 2 | What Should You Record for Every Answer? | Question and answer aren't enough. | Traces: inputs, steps, sources, timings | The log shows the answer, not the forecast it used | Planned |
| 3 | Which Numbers Should You Watch Every Day? | A few numbers tell you most of the story. | Metrics, dashboards, alerts | Wrong-answer complaints double on rainy days | Planned |
| 4 | Why Is It Slowly Getting Worse? | Quality can slide without anyone noticing. | Drift, online evaluation, sampling live answers | New city names, older weather sources | Planned |
| 5 | How Do You Connect a Complaint to What Happened? | A thumbs-down without context is a riddle. | Linking feedback to traces, replaying problems | "Wrong!" but which answer, which day? | Planned |
| 6 | So, Can You See Inside Your AI? | Five write-ups, one checklist. | Finale: the observability checklist | The weather bot, explained answer by answer | Planned |

## Format (same as #JustAddRAG)

- Curiosity-question titles, numbered from 1, "write-up" naming, no fixed schedule.
- Start with why the topic matters; plain-language analogies with technical names revealed at the end; light "Fix:" pointers.
- Polite humor, one very simple running example, cheat sheet at the end, three kickoff takeaways.
- Post text in a copy-paste block, with the "you pay for it" impact of the wrong choice.
- Cover: its own iceberg plus an original meme scene and a sticky-note punchline.
